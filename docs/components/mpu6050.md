# MPU6050 accelerometer and gyroscope

**Role in this project:** measures the machine's vibration (RMS, in milli-g) and its tilt from the installed
position. Vibration also reveals a stall: at a steady speed a turning motor always vibrates.

## What it is

A 6-axis MEMS motion sensor: a 3-axis accelerometer, a 3-axis gyroscope, a temperature sensor and 16-bit ADCs on
one chip, talking I2C at address 0x68 (AD0 low) or 0x69 (AD0 high). The GY-521 board adds a 3.3 V regulator and
pull-up resistors so it works from 5 V.

## How it senses

- **Accelerometer.** A tiny silicon proof mass hangs on spring beams. Acceleration moves it (F = m a, x = F / k).
  Interleaved comb fingers form capacitors, C = ε A / d: as the mass moves one gap closes and the other opens, and
  the difference in capacitance is proportional to acceleration. A differential pair cancels common errors such as
  temperature. It also feels gravity: at rest it reads 1 g.
- **Gyroscope.** A mass is kept vibrating; rotation adds a Coriolis force F = 2 m (v × Ω) at right angles to the
  vibration, sensed the same way. It outputs rotation rate in °/s. We do not use it.

| SDA | Arduino A4 (shared with the LCD) |

Fix the board to the same base as the motor: vibration travels through the structure.

## Settings (written straight to its registers, no library)

| Register | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0x6B PWR_MGMT_1 | 0x01 | Wake up (it powers up asleep), clock from the gyro's PLL |
| 0x1A CONFIG | 0x03 | Digital low-pass filter at about 44 Hz |
| 0x1C ACCEL_CONFIG | 0x08 | ±4 g full scale: 8,192 counts = 1 g, one count = 0.12 mg |
| 0x3B onward | read 6 bytes | X, Y, Z acceleration, high byte first |

±4 g instead of ±2 g gives headroom so a knock does not clip.

## How vibration is computed

1. Read the acceleration every 10 ms (100 Hz).
2. After 50 samples (0.5 s), compute each axis's variance: mean of squares minus square of the mean. Gravity is
   constant, so variance removes it and keeps only the shaking.
3. Add the three variances and take the square root: the RMS vibration in counts.
4. Convert: vibration (mg) = √variance / 8,192 × 1,000.

**Nyquist and the filter.** At 100 samples per second we can only see frequencies up to 50 Hz. The 44 Hz
low-pass filter removes faster vibration before it is sampled, so it cannot alias into a false low frequency.

## How tilt is computed

The average acceleration over a window points along gravity. Tilt is the angle between today's gravity vector
**a** and the one stored at installation **r**:

```
cos θ = (a · r) / (|a| |r|)
```

It works however the board is mounted. The `z` command stores the current direction as level. Warning at 10°,
stop at 25°, 2° of hysteresis.

**Why not the gyroscope?** An angle from a gyro means integrating its rate, and a small constant bias adds up into
drift. Gravity is an absolute reference while the machine stands still.

## Learned limits and stall detection

- After 2 s of spin-up at a new speed, 8 windows (4 s) are averaged into that speed's baseline. Warning = the
  larger of 2 × baseline and 40 mg; stop = the larger of 3.5 × baseline and 1.5 × the warning limit. Above 800 mg
  it always stops. Vibration must stay over the stop limit for 1 s to trip.
- If vibration at a steady running speed falls below 35 % of the baseline for 1.5 s, the shaft has stopped:
  emergency stop "MOTOR STALLED". Only checked when the baseline is at least 15 mg, well above sensor noise.

## Health checks

- At start-up the chip must answer at 0x68 or 0x69, otherwise FAULT.
- A real sensor always has some noise. Exactly zero variance for three windows means frozen data: FAULT.
- If it stops answering (for example a jammed I2C bus), it is retried only five times a second so time-outs cannot
  slow the safety loop.

## Limitations

- A geared motor's armature turns much faster than its output shaft, so much of its vibration is above 50 Hz and
  filtered out; we mostly see output imbalance and a loose mount.
- Industry judges vibration severity as velocity in mm/s RMS (ISO 10816 / 20816), not acceleration.
- A baseline learned on an already-faulty machine treats the fault as normal.
